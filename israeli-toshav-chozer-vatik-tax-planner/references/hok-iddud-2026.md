# Hok Iddud 2026: the Israeli-source personal-exertion exemption

Statute: חוק עידוד עלייה לישראל וחזרה אליה (הוראת שעה), התשפ"ו-2026, enacted as sections 8 and 9 (Part D) of the 2026 Economic Efficiency Law. Its Section 6 sets commencement at 1 January 2026, applying from the date the person became resident; Circular 07/2026 says the provision entered into force on 31 March 2026, but Section 6 applies it from 1 January 2026. Implementation: ITA Income Tax Circular 07/2026 (5 July 2026). Both are in Reference Links. Nothing below is tax advice; the binding determination is the assessing officer's, in the annual return.

## Who qualifies (Section 2(a))

- An **oleh** who became an Israeli resident for the first time, or a **toshav chozer vatik** who became an Israeli resident again, in the window **5 November 2025 to the end of tax year 2026**.
- The test is the date the **center of life actually moved** to Israel. The landing date and the date of the Misrad HaKlitah certificate do not decide it. Circular 07/2026 says a gap between the administrative approval and the real move does not help a person whose center of life moved outside the window.
- A toshav chozer vatik counts for this law only if they meet Section 14(a) **and** hold a returning-resident certificate from the Ministry of Aliyah and Integration (Section 1). A regular toshav chozer (6 to 9 years abroad) does not qualify at all.
- Section 2(e): Section 14(b)(1) does not apply when fixing the residency date. Circular 07/2026 gives the example: someone who moved in January 2025 and elected an acclimation year is still treated as resident BEFORE the window and does not qualify; someone who moved during 2026 qualifies even if they elect an acclimation year.

## What is exempt

"Qualifying income" (הכנסה מזכה) is taxable **personal-exertion income under Section 2(1) or 2(2)**: business or profession income AND employment income, produced in Israel while resident. Self-employed income is covered, not only salary.

Where income is produced follows Ordinance Section 4A: business income where the income-producing activity takes place, professional income where the service is performed, employment income where the work is performed. So work done in Israel for a foreign client or employer is Israeli-source: Section 14 does not exempt it, and this law exempts it only up to the cap. Circular 07/2026 s.15 on mixed income: the part tied to activity abroad is exempt under Section 14, the part tied to activity in Israel only up to the cap for that tax year.

Excluded:
- "Other income" as defined in Section 62A(d): interest, dividends, rent, capital gains and the sale of assets or real estate.
- Income attributed from a transparent entity (partnership, family company, house company) in which the individual is a **substantial shareholder** (Section 88 definition). Circular 07/2026: a non-substantial holding does qualify (its example: an active partner holding 5%), a wholly-owned transparent entity also qualifies, and salary, management fees or consulting fees paid by a transparent entity are not excluded merely because of the payer. For this law only, a wallet company (חברת ארנק) is not treated as a transparent entity.

## Annual caps (Section 2(a)) and the 2026 proration (Section 2(d))

| Tax year | General cap | Income from a relative (Section 2(b)) |
|---|---|---|
| 2026 | 600,000 NIS (pro-rated) | 140,000 NIS (pro-rated) |
| 2027 | 1,000,000 NIS | 140,000 NIS |
| 2028 | 1,000,000 NIS | 140,000 NIS |
| 2029 | 350,000 NIS | 140,000 NIS |
| 2030 | 150,000 NIS | 150,000 NIS (Section 2(b) replaces only paragraphs (1) to (3)) |

- Both 2026 caps (general and relative) are pro-rated to the part of 2026 the person was resident; Section 2(d) refers to paragraph (a)(1) "or (b)". Circular 07/2026 computes it as days from becoming resident to year end divided by 365 (its example: resident from 1 July 2026, 183/365).
- "Relative" follows Section 88: spouse, sibling, parent, grandparent, descendant, spouse's descendant and the spouse of any of them; a sibling's descendant and a parent's sibling; and a body of persons held 25% or more by the person or a relative. A company wholly owned by the individual is carved out, so salary from your own partly-owned company is relative income and salary from your wholly-owned company is not. The combined exempt amount from a relative and from others in one year may not exceed the general cap (Section 2(c)).
- The exemption is waivable in whole or in part ("אלא אם כן ביקשו אחרת"), the same structure as Section 14(a).
- The caps total 3.1M NIS of EXEMPT INCOME over 2026-2030, not 3.1M NIS of tax.

## Getting the exemption during the year (Circular 07/2026)

Full entitlement is decided in the annual return. During the year:
- An employee applies for tax coordination (תיאום מס), and a self-employed person for reduced advance payments, on **Form 116ע** (instead of the regular Form 116). Attachments: the oleh or returning-resident certificate, the Population Authority entry/exit printout for 2016-2025 for the applicant AND the spouse, payslips or income statements, and the ITA simulator output. Declarations include that the center of life moved in the window, an intention to stay resident to year end, that the income is not from a relative, not from a business received as a gift, not attributed from a transparent entity the person substantially holds, and that no Bituach Leumi allowance was received in 2016-2025.
- The in-year cap is **300,000 NIS** for a person not registered as required to file an annual return, and **500,000 NIS** for one who is registered (a self-employed person with an open file is normally registered; the test is the registration status when applying). In 2026 these are also pro-rated (the circular's example: 150,000 or 250,000 for a person resident from 1 July 2026). The rest of the exemption is claimed in the annual return.
- Income from a relative can be exempted only in the annual return, never through tax coordination.
- Receiving the exemption through withholding does not remove any annual-return obligation the person has for other reasons (self-employment, a foreign employer, income above the regulatory threshold).

### The day-count pre-screen for in-year relief

For tax coordination the assessing officer tests the move only by days in Israel before 2026, for the individual AND the spouse:
- **90-day rule**: in each year 2016 to 2025, no more than 90 days in Israel.
- **Exception**: the 90-day rule may be exceeded in at most three years, none of them 2016 or 2025: in two years up to 182 days each, and in one further year up to 150 days.
- **425-day test**: for each year 2018 to 2025, the cumulative days in that year and the two years before it must be below 425.
- Days of entry and exit count as days in Israel. A spouse is tested only from the year the couple became spouses.

Failing the pre-screen blocks only the IN-YEAR relief. The person can still claim the exemption in the annual return by showing the center of life moved within the window.

## The foreign-company limb (Section 3)

Business income of a **foreign-resident body of persons** that is produced in Israel solely because of the personal exertion of a qualifying oleh or vatik is exempt in 2026 to 2030, unless the company asks otherwise. It does not apply where the individual is a **substantial shareholder** of that company, and for a transparent foreign entity it does not cover the share attributed to an Israeli-resident holder. This is relevant to a returnee who keeps working from Israel for a foreign employer that would otherwise have an Israeli-source profit.

## The 2028-2029 clawback (Section 4)

The law does not apply, **from its start**, to an individual who ceased to be an Israeli resident during tax year 2028 or 2029 AND stayed in Israel fewer than 75 days in one of those years. Both conditions are required: Circular 07/2026 s.14 says a temporary absence with fewer than 75 days, without severing the center of life, does not cancel the 2026-2027 benefit. Ceasing residency also triggers the Section 100A exit tax (see `israeli-relocation-abroad`). 2026 and 2027 carry no such presence test.

## Interplay with Section 14 and the reporting regime

- Section 5: the law does not derogate from Sections 14 and 97, so the foreign-income exemption continues alongside it.
- A vatik who became resident between 5 November 2025 and 31 December 2025 is inside the Hok Iddud window AND under the legacy reporting regime of Step 4, because Amendment 272's reporting duty applies only to residency starting on or after 1 January 2026.
