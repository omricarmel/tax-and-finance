# Israeli Freelancer Tax Deadline Calendar

## VAT Filing Schedule (Osek Murshe)

### Bi-Monthly Filing (Standard)
An osek murshe whose turnover in the determining year is up to **1,775,000 NIS** (figure from September 2025; re-indexed every 1 September under VAT Law s.126(ד), so confirm it after each 1 September) files every two months. The deadline is the **15th of the month following the reporting period**. Filers who report and pay online may pay by the **19th**. A refund report and a zero report (no activity) are due on the same dates.

| Reporting Period | Filing Deadline |
|------------------|-----------------|
| January-February | March 15 |
| March-April | May 15 |
| May-June | July 15 |
| July-August | September 15 |
| September-October | November 15 |
| November-December | January 15 (following year) |

### Monthly Filing
Businesses above 1,775,000 NIS, and anyone required to file the detailed 874 report, file every month (detailed-report filers by the 23rd, see below). The deadline is the **15th of the following month**.

| Reporting Month | Filing Deadline |
|-----------------|-----------------|
| January | February 15 |
| February | March 15 |
| ... | (15th of the following month) |
| December | January 15 (following year) |

### Filing Method
VAT reports are filed online via the Tax Authority portal (misim.gov.il) or through accounting software with direct submission capability.

## Bituach Leumi (National Insurance), Self-Employed

Self-employed freelancers pay Bituach Leumi monthly advances (mkdamot). Take the due dates from the user's Bituach Leumi payment sheet (דף תשלומים) or BL's published schedule (Kol Zchut points there rather than stating a day). A **bank** standing order (הוראת קבע בבנק) is debited on the **22nd** for the previous month; a credit-card standing order is charged on the date agreed with the card company. A bank standing order also lets the December payment count in the current year, so up to 52% of it is a recognised expense that year.

Advances can be corrected once per quarter (January-March, April-June, July-September, October-December) when income changed by at least 10%.

**Note:** These are monthly advance payments (mkdamot). The exact amounts are set by the Bituach Leumi assessment (shuma).

Payment is based on the annual assessment (shuma) from Bituach Leumi. The advance amount may be adjusted after the annual tax return is filed.

## Annual Tax Report (Doch Shnati / Form 1301)

For tax year 2025 (Tax Authority Form 1301 service page):

- **Individuals filing a non-online report:** 29 May 2026
- **Individuals required to file online:** 30 June 2026 (online filing is mandatory for most filers)
- **Accountant-represented filers:** often on a later schedule; confirm with the accountant
- **Tax year 2026 (filed in 2027):** the Tax Authority sets the dates each year and has not published them yet. Do not assume them.
- The annual report is filed with the Tax Authority (mas hachnasa) and includes all income, expenses, and deductions for the tax year
- **Do not confuse with the osek patur annual turnover declaration**, which is a separate filing to the VAT office due January 31. The income-tax annual report is a different document and has different deadlines.

## Advance Tax Payments (Mkdamot Mas Hachnasa)

Freelancers may be required to pay income tax advances (mkdamot) throughout the year. The frequency is set in the Tax Authority assessment letter and determines the deadline:

The standard deadline is the **15th of the month following the reporting period** for both monthly and bi-monthly filers. Paying online via the Tax Authority website extends the deadline to the **19th** (until 18:30). Bi-monthly payment requires prior approval in the mkdamot book.

### Monthly Filers
Deadline: **15th of the month following the reporting month** (for example, January advance due February 15; the 19th if paid online).

### Bi-Monthly Filers
Deadline: **15th of the month following the bi-monthly period** (the 19th if paid online).

| Period | Standard Deadline | Online Deadline |
|--------|-------------------|-----------------|
| January-February | March 15 | March 19 |
| March-April | May 15 | May 19 |
| May-June | July 15 | July 19 |
| July-August | September 15 | September 19 |
| September-October | November 15 | November 19 |
| November-December | January 15 (following year) | January 19 (following year) |

The advance amount is set by the Tax Authority based on the previous year's income. Payment coupons (shoverim) are sent at the beginning of the tax year. If a deadline falls on Shabbat or a holiday, it shifts to the next business day.

## Shabbat and Holiday Adjustments

- **Shabbat rule:** If a deadline falls on Saturday (Shabbat), it is **moved to Sunday**.
- **VAT statutory rule (s.67(ב1)):** if at least three rest days (holidays and chol hamoed included) fall in the five days before a VAT deadline, the deadline moves to the fourth weekday after them.
- **Ad-hoc deferrals:** the Tax Authority announces one-off deferrals (holidays, emergencies); check its announcements before each alert.
- **Holiday rule:** If a deadline falls on a Jewish holiday when the Tax Authority offices are closed, it is typically moved to the **next business day**.

### Key Holidays That May Affect Deadlines
- Rosh Hashana (ראש השנה): September/October
- Yom Kippur (יום כיפור): September/October
- Sukkot (סוכות): September/October
- Pesach (פסח): March/April
- Shavuot (שבועות): May/June
- Yom Ha'atzmaut (יום העצמאות): April/May

**Note:** Always verify specific dates each year, as Hebrew calendar holidays shift relative to the Gregorian calendar.

## Osek Patur Specific Dates

Osek patur freelancers have fewer filing obligations:
- **No VAT returns:** exempt from VAT filing
- **Annual VAT declaration:** by January 31 of the following year, report previous year's turnover to the VAT office (the 2025 declaration was deferred once, to 30 April 2026)
- **Bituach Leumi:** same monthly schedule as above
- **Annual tax report:** same deadline as other filers (Form 1301; tax year 2025: 29 May 2026 non-online, 30 June 2026 online), unless the user is esek za'ir (below)
- **Threshold monitoring:** track revenue throughout the year; if the annual threshold (122,833 NIS for 2026) is exceeded, must convert to osek murshe

## Esek Za'ir (Income-Tax Micro Business) Dates

Esek za'ir is an income-tax status, so it applies to an osek patur or an osek murshe within the turnover ceiling:
- **Tax coordination (תיאום מס):** online, by **25 December** of each year
- **Shortened annual report:** tax year 2025 by **31 August 2026**; later years by **31 March** of the following year
- Missing the shortened report turns the business into a regular one, with a full annual report and the other duties of a regular business
- In most cases no mkdamot are required

## Year-End Savings Deadlines (31 December)

Set an alert for **15 December** with year-to-date business income. All 2026 figures; they are revised each January.

| Item | 2026 figures |
|---|---|
| Pension tax benefit (s.45א credit + s.47 deduction) | Up to 16.5% of income. Deduction: deposits up to 11% of income, max 25,608 NIS. Credit (35%): deposits up to 5% of income, max 11,640 NIS for a preferred member (עמית מוטב) or 16,440 NIS otherwise, plus a further 0.5% of business income for someone who took no disability-insurance deduction (Ordinance s.45א(ו)). Qualifying income is capped at 232,800 NIS. The deposit that captures the full benefit depends on income and member status: have the pension fund or accountant compute it, and do not quote a ceiling as the amount to deposit |
| Mandatory self-employed pension | 4.45% up to half the average wage, 12.55% from half up to the full average wage (13,769 NIS/month); maximum about 14,044 NIS a year in total. Ages 21 to under 60; exempt if 55 on 1 Jan 2017 and for the first six months after first VAT registration. Measured on the same pension deposits as the tax benefit, not added to it |
| Keren hishtalmut | Deductible up to 4.5% of taxable business income and of 293,397 NIS (13,203 NIS); tax-exempt gains on deposits up to 20,566 NIS |

## Detailed VAT Reporting (Report 874)

For an osek murshe who is an **individual** with annual turnover above **500,000 NIS** (companies already from 9/2025):
- Applies from reporting period **1/2026**
- Lists each invoice individually; invoices of up to 5,000 NIS pre-VAT may be reported as a total
- Forces monthly reporting, due the **23rd** of the following month
- An individual whose 2025 input invoices were at least 90% invoices of up to 5,000 NIS (by value or by count) may ask the regional VAT office to defer the duty to **1 January 2027**

## Israel Invoice Allocation Numbers

A tax invoice (hashbonit mas) needs an allocation number (mispar haktza'a) for the buyer to deduct input VAT (VAT Law s.38(א1)) when its pre-VAT amount is above the threshold in force on its **issue date**: 25,000 NIS in 2024 (pilot year), 20,000 NIS from 1 January 2025, 10,000 NIS from 1 January 2026, 5,000 NIS from 1 June 2026 (current). Keep the two statutory rules apart. The **buyer's** input-VAT deduction is barred without a number (VAT Law s.38(א1)). The **seller's** duty is s.47(א2)(1): an osek murshe may request a number for any tax invoice, and must request one when the buyer demands it and the pre-VAT amount exceeds the s.38(א1) threshold; it does not apply to zero-rated invoices. A number may also be requested after the invoice was issued (s.47(א2)(4)), but that does not extend the buyer's deduction period under s.38(א). The full number the system returns is long (the API field holds up to 30 characters; the spec's examples run 26 digits), and the Tax Authority recommends printing only its rightmost 9 digits, which is also the form reported in the detailed VAT report. Accept either form and never reject a number for its length. The Tax Authority's own guide calls it a 9-digit number; that is the printed short form.
