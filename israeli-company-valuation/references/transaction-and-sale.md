# Transaction and Sale Reference

Everything here applies when a REAL share sale or transfer sits behind the valuation.

The valuation is a price input, not the deal. Do not compute the seller's tax here, but do NOT let them walk away thinking the range is what they pocket. State each of the following, then send them to their accountant.

**The range is pre-tax.** An individual's real gain on shares bought on or after 1 January 2003 is generally taxed at 25%. That rises to 30% where the seller held 10% or more of any means of control at the date of sale or in any of the 12 preceding months, so anyone selling a meaningful stake in their own company is almost always in the 30% band, not the 25% one. On top of that, a 3% surtax applies to annual taxable income from all sources above ILS 721,560 in 2026, and as of 2025 a further 2% surtax applies to the portion of capital-source income, including capital gains, above that threshold. Ask for the original cost of the shares (מחיר מקורי), since without it nothing can be said about the after-tax outcome. Shares acquired before 2003 carry different apportionment rules, which matters for any long-held company.

**The sale itself creates duties with a short clock.** Do not state form numbers or deadlines from memory, but do tell the user these exist so they can act:

- The seller must report the sale and pay an advance on the tax within a short statutory window after the sale, separate from the annual return.
- The buyer or payer generally withholds tax at source from the consideration unless the seller obtains a certificate from the tax authority exempting or reducing withholding. This determines how much cash actually arrives at closing.
- The company must update its shareholder register and file the change with the Registrar of Companies (רשם החברות).

**A sale between existing shareholders is a related-party transaction.** Transfer pricing rules (section 85א of the Ordinance and the Income Tax (Determination of Market Conditions) Regulations, 2006) bite on an international transaction between parties with a special relationship, which must be reported at market conditions and needs a transfer pricing study; the benchmarking study itself is out of scope here. A purely domestic sale between partners is not therefore unexamined. A price materially away from market between related parties invites recharacterisation, so documentation matters more here, not less. The Companies Law approval track for a transaction with a personal interest governs transactions OF THE COMPANY, so it engages when the company itself buys back the shares or guarantees or funds the buyer's purchase, not on a plain sale between two shareholders, where the transfer restrictions in the articles and the shareholders' agreement govern instead. Confirm the approval route with the company's lawyer. For anything non-trivial, an advance tax ruling is the practical route, and that is true for a plain related-party share transfer, not only for a Section 104 reorganization.

**Share sale versus asset sale is a real fork.** Selling shares taxes the shareholder once; selling the activity or assets taxes the company and then again on extraction. Three more differences decide which side prefers which: an asset sale is a VAT transaction for the selling company, a buyer of assets gets a depreciable cost base at the price paid while a buyer of shares inherits the company's existing base, and real estate among the assets triggers betterment tax for the selling company and purchase tax for the buyer. This is usually the first structuring question in an Israeli private-company deal, and it changes the answer to what should we sell, not just what is it worth.

**Value is not price.** Flag the mechanism items that move cash more than a point of multiple does: locked-box versus completion accounts, net-debt and working-capital adjustments, deferred consideration and earn-outs (which must be discounted to present value, not added at headline), escrow or holdback against warranties, release of the seller's personal guarantees to banks and suppliers, and whether the seller stays on and at what salary. That last one loops straight back into the Step 3 normalization, so close the loop rather than leaving it open.

**Who values.** When two partners sit on opposite sides, decide early whether there is one jointly appointed expert, one per side, or an agreed third, and who pays. This is the practical first decision in a partner buyout.

## If the company holds real estate

If the company owns property, flag that a licensed appraiser is needed for it, and check whether the holding makes the company a real estate association (איגוד מקרקעין) under section 1 of the Real Estate Taxation Law. That reclassification is a tax characterisation question, not an appraisal question, and it matters: a sale of SHARES in such a company is an action in an association, charged to betterment tax under the Real Estate Taxation Law instead of ordinary capital gains, with purchase tax landing on the BUYER by reference to the association's real estate. The individual's headline rates do not change (section 48A(b) charges real betterment at up to 25%, rising to 30% for a substantial shareholder at the date of the action or in the preceding twelve months), so do not tell the seller the rate is different. The surtax layers ride on it too, since real betterment is charged as under section 121 of the Ordinance, so the top slice reaches 35% exactly as on an ordinary share sale. What changes is the charging statute, the buyer's purchase tax, the reporting track, and the loss of ordinary capital-gains apportionment. Cash and securities that do not serve to produce the company's income are disregarded in the test, so a cash-rich property company can still fall inside it.

## If the user is the BUYER

Everything above is written from the seller's side. A buyer of shares needs a different list:

- **A share purchase inherits the company, history included.** The buyer gets no step-up in the company's asset base, and the company's past tax positions travel with it: open shareholder debit balances under section 3(ט1), income attributed under section 62א, an unpaid 2% charge under sections 81א to 81ו. Each is a due-diligence question and, where found, a price or indemnity question.
- **Warranties and indemnities** for pre-closing tax and liabilities, backed by escrow or a holdback, are what make that history the seller's problem rather than the buyer's.
- **A non-compete and a transition period from the departing partner.** In a two-partner company part of the profit may walk out with the seller's relationships. Without a restraint or a handover, that part should not be in the price at all.
- **The buyer usually takes over the seller's personal guarantees** to banks and suppliers. Ask whether the lenders will release them.
- **Financing.** Personal funds, bank debt, or the company's own cash change the risk the buyer carries and, in the last case, the legal route (next section).

## Funding the buyout through the company

In Israeli partner buyouts the company often buys the departing partner's shares instead of the remaining partner. Under the Companies Law a purchase by the company of its own shares, and also the company FINANCING someone else's purchase of them, is a "distribution", the same category as a dividend, so it must pass the profit test (out of profits) and the solvency test (no reasonable concern that it will prevent the company from meeting its existing and expected liabilities when they fall due). A court may approve a distribution that fails the profit test, but only where the solvency test is met. That caps how much the company can pay. The tax cost can land on the partner who stays. In ע"א 9308/20 (בית חוסן) a majority of the Supreme Court held that in a private company that is a quasi-partnership, and many closely held two-partner companies may qualify as one (that is a legal determination for the company's lawyer; do not decide it here), each remaining shareholder is taxed on a dividend equal to the company funds used for the buyback multiplied by their holding percentage just before the transaction (in a 50/50 company paying X, the staying partner is taxed on about X/2), and is then treated as buying the seller's shares; the majority added that this result is consistent with the first alternative in Income Tax Circular 2/2018. The majority did not decide how the seller is taxed: the dissent points to a capital gain, and commentators differ, so confirm the seller's treatment with the accountant. So the remaining partner can owe dividend tax on money they never received, and a buyback is not automatically cheaper than buying with personal or borrowed funds. Further proceedings followed the ruling, so settle the current position with the accountant and, for anything material, an advance tax ruling, before anyone quotes a price on that basis.

## Retained earnings in the price

A company that has earned well for years usually holds accumulated profits. The equity bridge adds surplus cash at face, but taking that cash out costs the shareholder dividend tax, so practitioners settle either a pre-closing dividend or a price adjustment rather than leaving it implicit. Where the seller is a COMPANY, section 94ב taxes the part of the real gain that matches its share of the distributable profits at the rate that would have applied had it been received as a dividend just before the sale, which changes the seller's after-tax result and therefore what they will accept.

## Section 104 reorganizations and reported share transfers

Section 104 (Part E2 of the Income Tax Ordinance) lets an owner transfer an asset to a company for an allotment of shares without that transfer being a taxable sale at the time. The relief is a deferral, not an exemption: the tax follows the asset and lands on the eventual sale.

The valuation is not the end product. It fixes the share allocation ratio and the carried-over original cost of the shares allocated. A missing valuation basis is a named cause of disputes with the Israel Tax Authority and of failure to meet the value ratio requirement, so a thin or undocumented valuation is itself the risk here.

These reorganizations carry restriction periods and continuity conditions, and breaching them can retroactively unwind the deferral. **Do not state a specific holding period, continuity percentage, form number, or deadline from memory.** Those conditions were materially amended in 2025 and published summaries disagree about what currently applies. The remaining requirements are set out below.

- For anything non-trivial, the practical acceptance route is an advance tax ruling from the Israel Tax Authority with the valuation attached, not self-assessment defended later.
- Where the transfer is cross-border or between related parties, transfer pricing rules impose an arm's length requirement plus documentation.
- Confirm current form numbers and filing deadlines directly against the Israel Tax Authority.
